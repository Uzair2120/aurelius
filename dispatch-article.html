How Compound Interest Really Works (The 8th Wonder of the World)

By Aurelius Education Team • 5 Min Read • Beginner Friendly

Albert Einstein famously called compound interest the "eighth wonder of the world," adding that "he who understands it, earns it; he who doesn't, pays it." But what does it actually mean for your personal bank account?

1. Simple Interest vs. Compound Interest

When you earn simple interest, you only earn profit on your original deposit. For example, if you deposit $1,000 at 5% interest, you earn $50 every year. In 10 years, you'll have earned $500.

Compound interest is entirely different. In Year 1, you earn $50 on your $1,000. But in Year 2, you earn 5% on $1,050. That means you earn $52.50. In Year 3, you earn 5% on $1,102.50.

Over the first couple of years, the difference looks small. But as time goes on, the line turns into an upward hockey stick. You start earning interest on your interest, creating a snowball effect of pure free growth.

💡 The Rule of 72: A Quick Trick

Want to know how fast your money will double? Divide 72 by your expected annual interest rate.

If your investments grow at 7% per year: 72 ÷ 7 = ~10.2 years.
That means your money doubles every 10 years automatically without adding an extra cent!

2. A Tale of Two Savers: Sarah vs. Jordan

To see why starting early matters so much, look at Sarah and Jordan:

  • Sarah (Starts at age 25): Saves $300 every month into a diversified index fund earning an average 7% per year. She saves for just 10 years (until age 35, depositing a total of $36,000), and then stops adding new money, letting it grow until retirement at age 65.
  • Jordan (Starts at age 35): Waits 10 years to start. He then saves $300 every month for 30 consecutive years (from age 35 to 65, depositing a total of $108,000).

Who ends up with more money at age 65?

Surprisingly, Sarah ends up with over $340,000, while Jordan ends up with roughly $335,000 — even though Jordan put in $72,000 more of his own cash! Sarah won simply because compound interest had 10 extra years to do the heavy lifting.

3. Three Simple Rules to Maximize Compounding

  1. Start today, even with $50: Don't wait until you have a huge lump sum. Time in the market is far more powerful than timing the market.
  2. Automate your deposits: Set a recurring monthly transfer right after payday so you never forget or spend the money.
  3. Avoid unnecessary fees: High 1% or 2% advisor fees silently eat away your compounding returns. Keep your fees low and transparent.
